Mission

Supporting Vermont before the next outage.

Vermont's geography, weather, and aging infrastructure make it a strong fit for low-cost, decentralized communications. A mesh network can connect neighbors, volunteers, and responders even when the normal systems fail.

The first deployment centers on the University of Vermont campus, where a solar-powered RAK WisMesh Repeater Mini can become a durable backbone node for the surrounding community.

What the network is for

  • Off-grid text messaging and local coordination
  • Community resilience during storms and outages
  • Support for emergency planning and mutual aid
  • Open participation for residents and volunteers

How it works

No towers, no internet, no subscription.

Meshtastic and MeshCore turn inexpensive LoRa (Long Range) radios into a decentralized, peer-to-peer mesh. Every device that hears a message rebroadcasts it, so the network's reach grows with every new node — no cell towers, no ISPs, and no monthly bill.

Each person carries a small radio paired to their phone over Bluetooth. Fixed, elevated repeater nodes stitch coverage together across a city or valley. When internet is available, a node can bridge the local mesh to the global Meshtastic network — when it's not, the radio mesh keeps working entirely on its own.

First node

Votey Building at UVM as a launch point.

Proposed deployment

The inaugural node will be placed at the Votey Building on the University of Vermont campus. A solar-powered RAK WisMesh Repeater Mini would be mounted on a pole to create a reliable, elevated communications relay for nearby neighborhoods and campus users.

  • Elevated placement improves range and line-of-sight
  • Solar power supports continuity during grid outages
  • Open-source hardware keeps the deployment affordable and adaptable

Why this matters

A single well-placed node can strengthen local coverage, help new users discover the mesh, and establish a credible foundation for future expansion into Burlington and beyond.

Join the mesh

From unboxing to a live node in under an hour.

  1. 1

    Get a device

    Any Meshtastic-compatible LoRa radio works — handhelds start around $35–45.

  2. 2

    Flash the firmware

    Use the browser-based Meshtastic flasher — takes about five minutes per device.

  3. 3

    Pair the app

    Install the free Meshtastic app and connect over Bluetooth.

  4. 4

    Join BTV-Mesh

    Set your region to US and configure the public BTV-Mesh channel — no registration required.

  5. 5

    Show up on the map

    Enable MQTT on a WiFi-connected node and it will appear on meshmap.net.

Community growth

Built to grow organically.

The current community already reports roughly 20–30 visible nodes in and around Burlington, with links reaching toward Plattsburgh, Montreal, and New Hampshire. That makes Vermont well-positioned to build a practical regional mesh that can expand from campus and neighborhood adoption into a broader resilience network.

Deployment approach

  • Start with a visible, reliable anchor node
  • Publish simple joining instructions and channel guidance
  • Let community participation expand naturally
  • Document lessons for future Vermont partners

Redundancy and resilience

Mesh networks have already proven useful in real U.S. disasters.

Hurricane Helene, western North Carolina

After Helene disrupted power and communications across the region, local groups and community networks used Meshtastic-based tools to support coordination when mobile networks were unstable or down.

Sources: NC DPS, NC Mesh, FCC / telecom analysis

Winter Storm Uri, Texas

During the 2021 Texas power crisis, Austin Mesh was organized around a clear goal: provide a text-messaging layer that could work without cellular towers, internet, or grid power.

Source: Austin Mesh

Texas floods and mutual-aid response

In later flood events, community mesh operators documented the use of off-grid radio communications for mutual aid when commercial networks were degraded or unavailable.

Questions

Frequently asked questions.

Do I need a ham radio license?

No. Standard Meshtastic and MeshCore operation on 915MHz falls under FCC Part 15 — the same unlicensed category as WiFi routers and Bluetooth devices.

Does the mesh work without cell service or internet?

Yes — that's the whole point. Devices talk directly to each other over LoRa radio and rebroadcast messages for one another, so the network keeps working even when cellular, internet, and grid power are all down.

What does it cost to join?

A personal handheld device runs $35–100. There's no subscription, no account, and no registration — install the free app, flash the firmware, and join the public BTV-Mesh channel.

Is my traffic private?

All channels use AES-256 encryption, and direct messages between two devices are end-to-end encrypted.

How can UVM students get involved?

This project is designed around ORCA (Open Research Community Accelerator) semester engagements, with open project areas in network deployment, GIS coverage modeling, public dashboards, and emergency knowledge systems. Reach out through VERSO's GitHub to get involved.
